# Dish Washing Simulator

## Concept
The player is in a room with no interactables except for a sink with dirty dishes. The player is prompted to wash dishes, but the dishes clip in and out of the player’s hands or “float” away, unable to be cleaned.

## What is Bad Game Feel?
A successful game feel has the right combination of haptic feedback, controllability, and satisfying gameplay–”if a game designer has done his or her job correctly, the player will never notice the feel of a game. [...] it’s an “invisible art” (Swink, 2008). Modern games tend to measure this feeling with responsiveness (West, 2008), or the time the game takes to respond–like a response in a conversations, it can come too early or too late. But what defines what is “too” anything? With these vague definitions in mind, the term “bad game feel” could be used to describe when the response to an input is unexpected, resulting in glitches or other errors. Specific examples of this could include lagging or slow-to-respond buttons, uncontrollable objects, or items and objects clipping in and out of each other defying real physics.

## How Does This Portray Bad Game Feel?
By incorporating unexpected interactions, clipping, and incorrect collision boxes into the gameplay, the player is led to believe the game is “unfair”. The goal of this game is to be a sort of “No Fun” game, such as is discussed in Video Games Have Always Been Queer, where the goal is to make the player feel something besides happiness or accomplishment upon playing. This game ends, or doesn’t end, with an unsatisfying and open nature of a restart button, which also adds to this “No Fun” experience.

## Main Features
- Intercom voice telling the player to wash the dishes: “just do the dishes”, “it’s not that hard”, “it’s a choice to do the dishes, you’re just choosing not to do them”, “you’re overreacting” 
- Washing dishes, though the collisions clip, break, and the interactions are unexpected 
- Throwing dishes across room and them breaking 
- No satisfying “end”, just a reset button

## Project Inspirations (inconclusive)
- Realistic Kissing Simulator. This indie game, which I read about in Video Games Have Always Been Queer by Bonnie Ruberg, is a queer critique on status quo games and gameplay. It has no perceived timeline (you can end the game playing for any length of time), and the gameplay is quite simple, even mundane (each player manipulates an avatars tongue to engage in ‘kissing’). I want to emulate the unclear timeline and sense of player determined finishedness and use simplistic concepts and mechanics in order for players to read into interpretation.
- Anatomy. This game by Kitty Horrorshow on itch.io is a cult classic horror game that could or could not be a metaphor for anything and everything. The game is a sort of walking simulator, where the goal is to collect tapes from different rooms in a house and play them in the recorder. The tapes talk about the house as if it were a person with organs, and as the game progresses, the tapes and geometry of the house become more distorted and horrific. I loved the use of floating items and disarray as the house deteriorated to create unease. This game also had no satisfying ending, instead this game ends with the player stuck in the floor, unable to do anything but look up at a tape recorder. As if the house itself had trapped you. I want to create a similarly uneasy, or “unconventional” feeling game, that doesn’t lean towards player satisfaction or happiness, but instead stands alone with it’s message.
